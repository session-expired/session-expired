How to Play

The Objective

Somewhere inside Ward 9, a patient was murdered. Every player is a detective — there's no hero and no villain, just a room full of people trying to reach the truth before anyone else does.

Move through the ward, gather what other players are forced to reveal, and narrow down three facts: who did it, how, and where. The first player to march into the Warden's Office and name all three correctly wins.

The Board

  • 30 columns × 24 rows, 1 unit per square
  • Warden's Office: 8×9, fixed in the center
  • 4 corner rooms (two 9×7, two 9×8)
  • 4 edge rooms (two 8×5, two 8×6)
  • 8 spawn points located in the hallways
  • Secret passages connect NE↔SW and NW↔SE corners, free to use with no movement cost
Hallway squares can only hold one player at a time — you can't move onto or through an occupied hallway square. Rooms have no limit; any number of players can share a room.

Setup

2–4 players is standard, with room to stretch to 3 on the low end or 6 on the high end.

  • 6 suspects
  • 6 weapons / causes of death
  • 9 locations

One suspect, one weapon, and one location are set aside at random as the solution. Everything else is dealt out evenly among players. Turn order is assigned randomly at the start.

No physical cards are used — suspects, weapons, and locations are tracked as names or simple drawings in each player's journal (or as items in an inventory, if your version uses that).

The Warden's Office is never part of the solution pool — it's only where you go to make your final accusation.

Your Journal

Each player starts with a journal listing whatever suspects, weapons, and locations they were dealt.

  • Anything shown to you during a disprove is logged automatically.
  • Each guess gets its own entry: what you guessed, who showed you something (and what), or a note that nobody could show you anything.

Taking Your Turn

Movement

  • Roll a number to move.
  • You must use the full roll — no partial movement.
  • Each square costs 1 point of movement; entering or exiting a room also costs 1 point.
  • Secret passages are free to use.

Entering a Room

Entering a room ends your movement for the turn. A "Guess" option becomes available — you can guess or simply end your turn. If you're still in that room next turn, you can guess again without rolling. If you choose to roll instead, you give up your chance to guess that turn.

You get one guess per turn. You're allowed to reference cards you already hold yourself when guessing.

Making a Guess

A guess names a suspect and a weapon/cause of death. The location is automatically whatever room you're currently standing in — you must be physically in the room to name it.

Self-guessing is allowed — you can name yourself as the suspect or name a weapon/location you're holding.

The Disprove

After a guess, players are checked in turn order. The first player holding a matching suspect, weapon, or location card must show one of them — no bluffing or refusing. If they hold multiple matches, they choose which one to reveal.

Only the first matching player is asked; no one further down the order is approached, even if they also hold a match.

The player disproving gets about 10 seconds to show the card, which is then logged in the guesser's journal. Everyone at the table sees the reveal window open, whether or not a card is actually shown — so no one else can tell if information was exchanged.

If nobody has a match, that's also logged — it's a useful clue on its own.

Making an Accusation

Your final accusation — naming the full solution — can only be made from inside the Warden's Office.

Optional rule: instead of one wrong accusation ending your game, allow 2 (at most 3) accusations, with each wrong guess costing "sanity." Losing all your sanity eliminates you. 2 is the recommended max.

Winning & Losing

Correctly name the suspect, weapon, and location while in the Warden's Office to win. A wrong accusation eliminates you from guessing further — being the last player left doesn't win by default; you still have to guess correctly.

If every player guesses wrong (or runs out of sanity), the game ends with no winner, and the solution is revealed.
